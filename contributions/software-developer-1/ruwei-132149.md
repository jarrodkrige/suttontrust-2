Below is a **practical, beginner‑friendly animation plan** for the FirstGen site, focused on **scrolling, feedback, and reassurance** rather than flashy motion. These are designed to feel **calm, encouraging, and accessible for UK sixth‑formers**.

I’ve grouped them by **what the user experiences** and included **implementation guidance** (CSS/JS‑light, opt‑out with reduced‑motion).

***

## Animation Principles (important context)

Before specifics, all animations should:

*   Be **subtle** (≤300ms, small distances)
*   Have a **clear purpose** (orientation, emphasis, reassurance)
*   Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`
*   Never block reading or interaction

This keeps the site welcoming rather than overwhelming.

***

## 1. Scroll‑Triggered Section Reveals

### Animation

Sections gently **fade + slide up** when they enter the viewport.

**Effect**

*   Opacity: `0 → 1`
*   TranslateY: `16px → 0`
*   Duration: `250–350ms`
*   Easing: `ease-out`

**Applies to**



**Why it helps**

*   Gives users a sense of progress while scrolling
*   Reduces “wall of text” fatigue
*   Makes the site feel cared‑for and modern

**Implementation hint**

*   Use `IntersectionObserver`
*   Add a `.reveal` class on entry
*   Disable under `prefers-reduced-motion`

***

## 2. Numbers Section: Count‑Up Animation

### Animation

Statistics count up from `0` when scrolled into view.

**Effect**

*   Numeric increment
*   Duration: `600–900ms`
*   Ease: `ease-out`

**Applies to**



**Why it helps**

*   Draws attention to impact
*   Improves recall of key facts
*   Feels rewarding without being distracting

**Accessibility**

*   Respect reduced motion
*   Ensure stat values are still readable without animation

## 3. Navigation: Scroll Feedback

### 3.1 Active Section Highlight (Scroll‑Spy)

**Animation**
Active nav link gains:

*   Colour change
*   Bottom underline slides in (`scaleX`)

**Why it helps**

*   Users always know where they are
*   Especially helpful on long, single‑page layouts

***

### 3.2 Smooth Anchor Scrolling

**Animation**
Scrolling to sections is animated rather than jumping.

```css
html {
  scroll-behavior: smooth;
}
```

**Why it helps**

*   Less jarring
*   Stronger sense of spatial continuity

***

## 4. Hero & CTAs: Reassuring Feedback

### 4.1 Primary CTA Hover / Focus

**Animation**

*   Slight scale up (`1 → 1.03`)
*   Soft shadow appears
*   Duration: `150–200ms`

**Applies to**



**Why it helps**

*   Confirms clickability
*   Makes the action feel safe and intentional

***

### 4.2 CTA Press State

**Animation**
On click/tap:

*   Button compresses slightly (`scale: 0.98`)
*   Returns on release

**Why it helps**

*   Gives tactile feedback, especially on mobile
*   Reduces “did it work?” anxiety

***

## 5. Voices Section: Gentle Engagement

### 5.1 Voice Card Hover (Desktop)

**Animation**

*   Card lifts slightly (`translateY(-4px)`)
*   Shadow increases
*   Quote icon fades in

**Why it helps**

*   Encourages exploration without forcing interaction
*   Feels human and inviting

***

### 5.2 Voice Carousel (Mobile Only)

**Animation**

*   Horizontal swipe
*   Snap points
*   Small pagination dots fade in/out

**Why it helps**

*   Prevents tiny text cards
*   Natural interaction for phone users

**Effect**

*   Height auto‑expands
*   Opacity transition for new content

**Why it helps**

*   Encourages curiosity while keeping pressure low

## 7. Resources Section: Orientation Animations

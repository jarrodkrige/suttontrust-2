First‑generation students are not rare. UK data consistently shows that a large proportion — often around half to two‑thirds — of university students or graduates are the first in their family to attend university. Being first‑generation is a common experience, not an exception.
(UCAS; UCL/Nuffield Foundation; Office for Students)

1) “About two‑thirds of graduates are first‑generation”

Claim supported: First‑generation students are not an unusual minority.
Evidence:
UCAS states that almost two‑thirds of graduates are first‑generation students (defined as having no parent or carer who attended university).
Source & year: UCAS (page current 2023–2024)
Citation: UCAS, First‑generation students [ucas.com]

3) Office for Students: first‑in‑family is a major regulated equality group

Claim supported: First‑generation students are numerous enough to be a national regulatory priority.
Evidence:
The Office for Students (England’s higher‑education regulator) treats “first in family” as a core student characteristic in its Equality of Opportunity Risk Register, supported by HESA and UCAS data.
Source & year: Office for Students, 2024
Citation: OfS, First in family – student characteristic [officefors...nts.org.uk]
